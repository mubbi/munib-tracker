# Proof log: voluntary fasting tracker (NF-3.2, D11)

- **Feature:** plan, log and remind recommended voluntary fasts; label disliked days; block the prohibited ones; link a kept fast to roza qaza.
- **Code:** the classifier is `packages/shared/src/utils/fasting-calendar.ts` (`classifyFastDay`). Evidence refs are in `packages/shared/src/content/voluntary-fasting-evidence.ts`. App wiring is in `apps/app/src/lib/voluntary-fasting/*`, the hub is `apps/app/src/app/fasting/index.tsx`, and the copy is in `apps/app/scripts/i18n/voluntary-fasting-i18n.json` (`voluntaryFasting.*`).
- **Review:** AI proof-backed review (D11), 2026-11-23.
- **Scripture:** none is typed. The UI shows short guide paraphrases plus citation labels; hadith text is only ever rendered from the corpus.
- **Hijri dates:** every date comes from `lib/hijri.ts` (moon-sighting authority plus the user's adjustment, D14).
- **UI disclaimer:** "For your own situation, consult a qualified scholar." (`voluntaryFasting.disclaimer`)

## 2. Prohibited and disliked days (`classifyFastDay`, in order)

1. **Eid (1 Shawwal, 10 Dhul-Hijjah):** prohibited, and logging a fast is blocked. Bukhari 1990; Muslim 1137. This is agreed by all four schools (consensus).
2. **Tashriq (11–13 Dhul-Hijjah):** prohibited and blocked. Muslim 1141. All four schools prohibit voluntary fasting on these days. The narrow exceptions (e.g. the Maliki/Hanbali allowance for the tamattu' pilgrim without a hady) don't apply to a voluntary tracker. The 13th loses its White Day kind. The White Days surfaces (tracker section, home banner, White Days reminder) use the same Hijri gate (`voluntaryFastBlockForHijri`), so 13 Dhul-Hijjah is never suggested as a White Day and Ramadan's 13th–15th stay in the Ramadan log.
3. **Ramadan:** excluded. These days belong to the Ramadan store.
4. **After mid-Sha'ban (16th onward):**
   - **Shafi'i only:** **not permitted** (ruling `prohibited`, not blocking), unless the fast is habitual (Mon/Thu followed, or Dawud), joined day by day to fasting on or before the 15th, a Qaza fast or a vow. This follows `docs/proofs/madhhab.md` → `fasting.afterMidShaban`: the relied-upon view per Tuhfat al-Muhtaj and al-Majmu' is haram, not makruh. The plan's "disliked" was corrected.
   - **Hanafi, Maliki, Hanbali:** no specific rule.
   - **All views (`madhhab === null`):** the ruling is unchanged and a labelled note plus `MadhhabNote` is shown. Nothing is imposed.
   - **Vows:** these aren't tracked. The copy names them as an exception.
5. **Day of doubt (30 Sha'ban):** disliked unless it is a Qaza fast.
   - Evidence: Abu Dawud 2334 / Tirmidhi 686 (disputed grade).
   - **Schools differ:** Shafi'i: not valid without a reason (Minhaj). Hanafi: it isn't fasted except as a voluntary fast, and fasting it with a Ramadan intention is disliked (al-Hidayah: "la yusam yawm al-shakk illa tatawwu'an"). Maliki: a voluntary fast is allowed, a precautionary Ramadan fast is disliked. Hanbali: the school's well-known view makes fasting obligatory when cloud prevents sighting on the 30th night (Zad al-Mustaqni').
   - **Copy:** because of this disagreement, the copy says "scholars differ. Many dislike fasting it, especially as a precaution for Ramadan" and asserts nothing more.
6. **One or two days before 1 Ramadan:** disliked unless habitual or a Qaza fast. Evidence: Bukhari 1914; the exception is in the hadith's own wording.
7. **Friday singled out:** disliked when the user has no planned Thursday or Saturday, and the day is neither a customary fast (Arafah, Tasu'a, Ashura, a White Day, Dawud) nor a Qaza fast. The customary exception is the wording of Muslim 1144.02.
   - Evidence: Bukhari 1985; Muslim 1144.
   - **Schools:** Shafi'i: makruh (Minhaj: "wa yukrah ifrad al-jumu'ah"). Hanbali: makruh (Zad: "wa yukrah ifrad… al-jumu'ah"). Imam Malik saw no harm (al-Muwatta', Kitab al-Siyam). Within the Hanafi school the reports differ.
   - **Copy:** "Many scholars (the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools) dislike…; others allow it."
8. **Arafah for this year's pilgrim:** permitted, with a "not recommended" note. Evidence: Bukhari 1988. Most scholars hold that the pilgrim at Arafah should not fast. That is why the plan's "disliked per the majority" is shown as a note, not a ruling (plan rule 7). The "performing Hajj" answer is stored with the Hijri year of that Hajj (`pilgrimHijriYear`) and only applies in that year, so it expires by itself after that Dhul-Hijjah; Dhul-Hijjah mode (day-9 deed, Arafah view, Arafah reminders) honours it too.
9. **Otherwise:** any recommended kind gives `recommended`; with none, `permitted`.

## 3. Excused periods

- **Hayd:** a day can't be marked `fasted` (Bukhari 304). Intending, or logging a fast as broken, is still allowed. The copy is gentle: "There is no blame in this; Allah has excused it."
- **Nifas:** blocked exactly like hayd (plan audit 2026-11-24). The scholars agree that postnatal bleeding takes the rulings of hayd for fasting and Salah (consensus reported by Ibn Qudamah, _al-Mughni_, Kitab al-Hayd; al-Nawawi, _al-Majmu'_, Bab al-Hayd). No hadith number is cited in the app for this; the copy only says the day can't be marked as fasted. Every surface that logs a fast (`/fasting`, the tracker's White Days section, the Dhul-Hijjah deeds checklist) goes through the same `blockReasonFor` gate.
- **Illness and travel:** fasting may be logged. These excuses (Qur'an 2:184, already verified in the madhhab log) only allow breaking the fast.
- **Any excused period:** every fasting reminder is paused.

## 4. Qaza link and the order of Shawwal six

- **Qaza link:** a kept fast marked "count as Qaza" takes one day off the roza qaza counter. An idempotent `qazaCounted` flag (mirroring `PrayerLog.qazaDebtAdded`) means it never counts twice, and undoing it restores exactly one day.
- **Shawwal six while Qaza is owed:** the note "Many scholars advise making up Ramadan first; others allow either order" is informational only and never blocks logging. The basis for "either order": 'A'ishah delayed her make-up days until Sha'ban (Bukhari 1950). The Hanbali school links the Shawwal reward to completing Ramadan first; the others allow voluntary fasts before the make-up days. Because the schools differ, the copy says "scholars differ" in plain words and asserts no single view.

## 5. Notifications (privacy)

- **What a notification says:** titles and bodies only name the day ("Tomorrow: Day of Arafah") or the time ("Fajr is in 45 minutes"). They never mention hayd, Qaza amounts or any other private state.
- **Budget tiers (§11):** suhoor is tier 2; the night-before intent and the Arafah/Ashura nudges two days ahead are tier 3.
